## Abstract

NISQ processors corrupt both gates and measurements, biasing circuit expectation values. We build a controlled Qiskit Aer testbed comparing four mitigation strategies — full and tensored readout error mitigation (REM), zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE), and ZNE+REM — against a no-mitigation baseline, across 120 conditions (2/4/6 qubits, 2/4 layers, ideal/low/moderate/high course-standard noise, 5 seeds), and apply the same methods to a variational quantum classifier (VQC) on Iris binary, compared against Logistic Regression and SVM. At moderate noise, ZNE+REM gives the largest median error cut (68%); at low noise, full REM alone is more reliable, while ZNE alone shows no significant benefit (p = 0.984). On the VQC, mitigation restores decision margins sharply (margin retention at high noise: 0.411 unmitigated vs. 0.847 mitigated) while accuracy moves only slightly, showing accuracy is a coarse lens for judging mitigation. The exact VQC reaches accuracy comparable to LR and SVM on identical inputs; no quantum advantage is claimed. Tensored REM matches full REM at a fraction of the circuit cost, and variance-optimal ZNE shot allocation cuts the extrapolation's standard deviation by 0.874×, close to the 0.885× theory predicts.

## 1. Introduction, problem and objectives

### 1.1 Problem statement

Current NISQ processors are noisy. Gate errors and measurement (readout) errors bias the outputs of quantum circuits, and that bias grows with circuit width and depth. Quantum error mitigation reduces this bias through extra circuit executions and classical post-processing, without needing extra qubits, but it costs extra circuits, shots and depth, and it has known limits. This project investigates three things: whether selected mitigation methods improve the output quality of a noisy circuit; what each method costs; and whether expectation-value gains carry through to a downstream quantum machine-learning (QML) classification task.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **O1.** Build a controlled, reproducible noisy-simulation testbed in Qiskit Aer, using the course-standard noise levels.
- **O2.** Implement readout error mitigation (full and tensored) and zero-noise extrapolation (global folding with Richardson extrapolation), alone and combined.
- **O3.** Quantify how effective each method is (expectation-value error, distribution fidelity, success probability) and what it costs (circuits, shots, depth, two-qubit gates, time), across qubits, depth and noise.
- **O4.** Evaluate the same methods on a variational quantum classifier for Iris binary, against Logistic Regression and SVM baselines.
- **O5.** Propose cheaper mitigation variants and test them with data.

### 1.3 Research questions

- **RQ1.** Do REM, ZNE and their combination reduce expectation-value error and improve the output distribution of a noisy circuit across noise levels, qubit counts and depths?
- **RQ2.** What is the resource cost of each method, and how does it scale with qubit count?
- **RQ3.** Do expectation-value improvements translate into better classification metrics for a VQC on Iris binary? How does the VQC compare with LR and SVM on identical inputs?
- **RQ4.** Can cheaper variants (tensored REM, variance-optimal shot allocation for ZNE) keep most of the benefit at lower cost?

### 1.4 Scope: two tracks

**Track A** (the mitigation testbed) uses a layered `ry`-`rz`-CNOT ansatz on 2, 4 and 6 qubits at 2 or 4 layers, under four noise levels, 5 methods, 5 seeds — 600 result rows — and answers RQ1, RQ2 and RQ4. **Track B** (the QML application) trains a VQC on the Iris versicolor/virginica task at 2 or 4 qubits (2 PCA features or all 4), evaluates it under the same noise levels and mitigation methods, and compares it against Logistic Regression and SVM on identical preprocessed features and the identical train/test split, answering RQ3.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Track A circuit

Each instance has n qubits and L layers; layer ℓ applies `ry(θ)` then `rz(φ)` to every qubit, then a CNOT chain `cx(0,1), cx(1,2), …`. Angles are drawn uniformly from [0, 2π).

*D1. The ansatz for n = 4, L = 2.*

|---|---|---|---|

| 4 | 4 | 32 | 12 |

| 6 | 4 | 48 | 20 |

**Instance selection.** A random multi-qubit parity is often near zero, where the shot-noise floor (σ ≈ 0.031 at 1024 shots) hides every effect and relative error is undefined. Angles are redrawn from a seeded stream until |E_exact| ≥ 0.3. Across the 30 instances this took a median of 2 and at most 35 draws; selected |E_exact| range from 0.311 to 0.872. This is a selection bias, listed in §7.4.

### 3.3 REM: full and tensored

**Calibration.** For each of 2ⁿ basis states, a circuit prepares it with `ry(π)` gates (staying inside the noisy gate basis) and measures. Column j of the assignment matrix A is the measured distribution when state j was prepared; A[i,j] = P(measured i | prepared j), and every column sums to 1.

![D3](../results/figures/fig_D3_calibration_circuits.png)

*D3. Full calibration (2ⁿ circuits, n = 2 shown) vs. tensored calibration (2 circuits, independent of n).*

**Inversion.** q = A⁻¹p̃ gives a quasi-probability vector (sums to 1, may have negative entries). Expectation values are taken from the raw q (linear, unbiased by projection); where a valid distribution is needed (fidelity, TVD), q is projected onto the probability simplex by Euclidean projection (Smolin, Gambetta & Smith, ref. 17): sort descending, find the largest prefix whose threshold keeps every entry non-negative, and subtract that threshold, clipping at 0.

**Tensored REM** builds a per-qubit 2×2 assignment matrix from only 2 calibration circuits (all-|0⟩, all-|1⟩) and combines them as A = A_{n−1} ⊗ … ⊗ A₀, assuming uncorrelated readout errors — true by construction in our noise model.

### 3.4 ZNE

**Global folding.** For odd λ = 2k+1, U_λ = U(U†U)^k: the same ideal unitary, λ times the gates. `u.inverse()` negates every rotation angle and leaves `cx` unchanged, so folding never leaves the noisy gate basis.

![D2](../results/figures/fig_D2_folded_circuit.png)

*D2. Folding at λ = 3 (n = 2, L = 1): U, then U†, then U again.*

**Richardson extrapolation.** E₀ = Σᵢ γᵢE(λᵢ), γᵢ = Πⱼ≠ᵢ λⱼ/(λⱼ−λᵢ). For λ = (1, 3, 5), γ = (1.875, −1.25, 0.375), summing to 1. Because √Σγᵢ² ≈ 2.28, Richardson extrapolation amplifies the shot-noise standard deviation by about that factor — the central cost of ZNE (§6.3). Measurements are never folded, so readout error does not scale with λ, and ZNE alone cannot remove it (§6.1, H3).

### 3.5 Encoding and ansatz for the VQC

Track B encodes each feature as `ry(x_k)` on qubit k (angle encoding), giving ⟨Z_k⟩ = cos(x_k) before the ansatz. Features are scaled to [0, π] so the minimum value maps to |0⟩ and the maximum to |1⟩ with no wrap-around. By Schuld, Sweke & Meyer (ref. 15), a single encoding layer restricts the model to Fourier frequencies {−1, 0, +1} per feature — a real expressivity limit, not just an implementation detail, and the reason data re-uploading is listed as future work (§7.3). After encoding, the same layered `ry`-`rz`-CX block as Track A follows, with trainable weights.

![D4](../results/figures/fig_D4_vqc_circuit.png)

*D4. The VQC for n = 4, L = 2: angle encoding, then the trainable ansatz.*

| n | L | Trainable params | 1-qubit gates (incl. encoding) | CNOTs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 | 8 | 10 | 2 |
| 2 | 4 | 16 | 18 | 4 |
| 4 | 2 | 16 | 20 | 6 |
| 4 | 4 | 32 | 36 | 12 |

The model output is f(x) = ⟨Z^{⊗n}⟩ ∈ [−1, 1]; the prediction is sign(f(x)), with a tie (f = 0) going to +1. Training is deliberately noiseless and exact (COBYLA minimizing MSE against y ∈ {+1, −1}), which isolates the effect of noise and mitigation to inference time and avoids the noise-induced vanishing-gradient failure mode Wang et al. (ref. 16) prove for noisy training.

### 4.2 Key code

**Global folding** (`src/qem/circuits.py`):

```python
def fold_global(u: QuantumCircuit, scale: int) -> QuantumCircuit:
    assert scale >= 1 and scale % 2 == 1
    k = (scale - 1) // 2
    folded = u.copy()
    for _ in range(k):
        folded.barrier()
        folded.compose(u.inverse(), inplace=True)
        folded.barrier()
        folded.compose(u, inplace=True)
    return folded
```

**REM inversion** (`src/qem/mitigation/readout.py`): solve `A q = p̃` (or `lstsq` above a condition-number threshold), record the negative mass, and project only for distribution metrics — never for the expectation value itself.

**Noise model** (`src/qem/noise.py`): depolarizing errors attached to `ry`/`rz`/`cx` by gate name, plus a symmetric readout bit-flip; the executor asserts every transpiled circuit stays inside {`ry`, `rz`, `cx`, `measure`, `barrier`}, so no gate silently runs noise-free.

## 6. Comparative analysis

**RQ1 — does each method reduce error, by how much, and what happens at high noise?** At moderate noise every method helps: median improvements of 43% (rem), 48% (rem_tensored), 44% (zne) and 68% (zne_rem), all pooled-significant (Wilcoxon p = 1.9e-09, 1.9e-09, 3.7e-09, 4.7e-08). At low noise the picture is less uniform: REM still helps clearly (median 42%, p = 8.0e-08), but ZNE alone shows **no significant benefit** (median 1%, p = 0.984 — far from significant), because its variance amplification outweighs a small bias at this noise level. At the optional `high` stress level every method still helps relative to the unmitigated baseline (medians 29%, 25%, 19%, 58%), and `zne_rem` remains the strongest method; out-of-range Richardson estimates stayed rare even here (0 at `high` out of 0/120 overall for `zne`), though the exponential fit failed more often (10/120 for zne, 13/120 for zne_rem, pooled over all noise levels).

**RQ1 — qubits and depth.** `table_error.md` and A4 show error growing with both n and L for every method, most steeply for the unmitigated baseline. REM's relative benefit is largest at small n and shrinks as calibration becomes noisier at larger n; ZNE's relative benefit is more uniform across n but costs 9× the CNOTs regardless of size.

**RQ1 — readout vs. gate noise.** `table_bias_sources.md` splits the unmitigated bias exactly (using the noise-model exact density matrix): at low noise 60% of the bias is readout, falling to 40% at moderate noise as gate noise, which grows with circuit size, takes over. This matches the method comparison: REM (which fixes only readout) is relatively more valuable at low noise, ZNE (which fixes only gate noise, not readout — because folding never touches the measurement) is relatively more valuable at moderate noise, and only the combination addresses both.

**RQ2 — cost.** Per estimate: `none` needs 1 circuit; full `rem` needs 1+2ⁿ (5, 17, 65 circuits at n = 2, 4, 6 — confirming the 2ⁿ scaling); `rem_tensored` needs a constant 3 at any n; `zne` needs 3 circuits but 9× the CNOTs (9×, measured) and up to 5.00× the depth (95 gates at the deepest); `zne_rem` needs the sum. Simulator wall time is not hardware time, so circuit and shot counts, not seconds, are the meaningful cost figures (`table_resources.md`).

**RQ2 — ideal-noise sanity check.** With A = I at the ideal level, REM is an exact no-op (largest observed change: 0). ZNE, with no bias to remove, only adds shot-noise variance: it was worse than doing nothing in 26/30 of 30 runs.

**RQ2 — does ZNE amplify variance as predicted?** Measured against each estimate's own exact infinite-shot value (so the comparison contains shot noise only), the ZNE standard deviation is 2.38×, 2.42×, 2.70× and 2.07× the baseline's at the four noise levels, against a Richardson-weight theoretical prediction of 2.28× (`zne_std`'s own per-run prediction averaged 2.29×–2.32×). The measured ratios run somewhat above the √5.22 ≈ 2.28 theoretical figure, particularly at moderate and high noise, which points to the folded circuits' shots not behaving as perfectly independent ±1 draws at larger λ — plausible given the readout and residual gate correlations the simple variance model ignores; this is flagged as a limitation (§7.4).

**RQ3 — does mitigation improve VQC metrics, and does accuracy move as much as expectation error?** No: accuracy is far coarser. At high noise, unmitigated mean |E_hat − E_exact| is 0.301 and margin retention collapses to 0.411, while accuracy only drifts from 0.920 (ideal) to 0.900 (high). With `zne_rem`, mean error falls to 0.121 and margin retention recovers to 0.847, while accuracy is 0.903 — barely different from the unmitigated figure. With only 20 test samples, accuracy moves in 5% steps and cannot resolve differences that the continuous expectation-value and margin metrics show clearly (B4, B5). This directly supports H8.

**RQ3 — VQC vs. LR and SVM.** The exact (noiseless) VQC reaches a mean test accuracy of 0.920 (range 0.850–1.000 across n, L and seeds) against Logistic Regression's 0.920 (n=2 features) / 0.930 (n=4) and SVM's 0.920 / 0.910, on the identical split and identical preprocessed features (`table_qml_classification.md`). These are comparable, with no systematic gap in either direction; **no quantum advantage is claimed**. All three models were fit only on the training split and never saw the test labels; no hyperparameters were tuned on the test set.

**RQ4 — does tensored REM match full REM?** Yes, closely: paired by circuit instance and noise level, the mean absolute difference between full and tensored REM's error is 0.0063, with a worst case of 0.0388 (`table_rem_full_vs_tensored.md`, A12) — at a cost of 3 circuits instead of up to 65. This favors the tensored method because our noise model has uncorrelated readout errors by construction (§7.4).

**RQ4 — does variance-optimal shot allocation help?** Yes, and closely matching theory: at the same 3072-shot total budget, the optimal allocation (1646,1097,329 shots at λ = 1, 3, 5, over 50 repetitions per condition) gives a mean empirical standard deviation 0.874× the uniform allocation's, against a 0.885× theoretical prediction (`table_zne_allocation.md`, I1).

## 7. Limitations, research gap and proposed improvement

### 7.1 Research gap

As detailed in §2 and `report/literature_notes.md`: prior work mostly studies readout mitigation and extrapolation-based mitigation separately, at hardware scale, and rarely connects expectation-value gains to downstream QML metrics measured against classical baselines on identical inputs. This project's contribution is a small, fully controlled, cost-normalized comparison spanning both settings in one paired protocol.

### 7.2 Improvement 1: tensored REM

**Motivation.** Full calibration costs 2ⁿ circuits, infeasible past roughly 10–15 qubits. **Result:** tensored REM (2 circuits) matches full REM to within 0.0063 mean absolute error (§6, RQ4) — confirmed with data, not assumed. **Limitation:** our noise model has uncorrelated readout by construction, which favors the tensored method; real hardware may have correlated readout errors that this comparison does not test (Appendix A.4, optional, was not run).

### 7.3 Improvement 2: variance-optimal ZNE shot allocation

**Motivation.** Richardson extrapolation's large, mixed-sign weights amplify shot noise; uniform shot allocation (1024 per scale) is not variance-optimal for a fixed total budget. **Theory.** Minimizing Var(E₀) = Σγᵢ²σᵢ²/Nᵢ subject to ΣNᵢ = N gives Nᵢ ∝ |γᵢ|σᵢ; assuming σᵢ ≈ 1 (no pilot run needed), this gives 1646,1097,329 for λ = (1, 3, 5) at N = 3072, matching the closed-form prediction exactly. **Deviation note:** this experiment deliberately uses a shot allocation other than 1024 per circuit, while keeping the *total* budget (3 × 1024 = 3072) unchanged from the rest of the study; it is reported separately (`table_zne_allocation.md`) and never substituted into the main Track A grid. **Result:** the measured variance reduction (0.874×) closely tracks the theoretical prediction (0.885×).

### 7.4 Limitations

- **Mitigation is not correction.** It improves average expectation values only; individual shots, and in Track B individual predictions, stay noisy.
- **ZNE.** Amplifies variance (theory ≈ 2.28×; measured 2.42×–2.07×, somewhat above theory — see §6, RQ2). Its extrapolation model can mismatch the true decay (the exponential fit failed on 10/120 of 120 `zne` conditions). It assumes noise scales faithfully with folding, clean in this incoherent simulated model but less clean on hardware. It never removes readout error, because measurements are not folded. The deepest folded circuit reaches 95 gates (5.00× the base), which on real hardware risks exceeding coherence times. It shows no significant benefit at low noise (§6, RQ1).
- **REM.** Full calibration costs 2ⁿ circuits. It assumes readout noise is stable over time and independent of state preparation. It can produce negative quasi-probabilities (largest observed mass: 0.0186 full, 0.0169 tensored). It does nothing for gate noise. The tensored variant additionally assumes uncorrelated readout, untested against a correlated-noise stress case here.
- **Simulation only.** The depolarizing-plus-readout model is incoherent and Markovian; real devices add coherent errors, crosstalk, T1/T2 relaxation, drift and leakage.
- **Statistics.** Three qubit counts, two depths and 5 seeds per condition limit statistical power; the Track B test set has only 20 samples per seed, so accuracy moves in 5% steps.
- **Selection bias.** Track A instances are selected by |E_exact| ≥ 0.3, so they are not uniformly random circuits (§3.1).
- **VQC training.** Trained noise-free; a single encoding layer limits expressivity (§3.5, ref. 15); noise-aware training was not attempted, for the reason given in ref. 16 (§3.5).
- **Time.** Simulator wall time does not equal hardware execution time.

## 8. Conclusion

**RQ1.** Yes — REM, ZNE and their combination all reduce expectation-value error at moderate and high noise, with the combined method strongest overall. At low noise, REM (full or tensored) is the more dependable choice; ZNE alone shows no significant benefit there because its variance cost outweighs a small bias. **RQ2.** Costs scale predictably: REM as 1+2ⁿ circuits, ZNE as a flat 3 circuits but 9× the CNOTs and up to 5× the depth, and the combination as their sum; simulator time is not the relevant hardware-cost proxy. **RQ3.** Expectation-value gains are real and visible in continuous metrics (mean error, margin retention), but barely move classification accuracy, which is too coarse a lens with only 20 test samples; the exact VQC is comparable to, not better than, Logistic Regression and SVM on identical inputs, and no quantum advantage is claimed. **RQ4.** Yes to both proposed improvements: tensored REM matches full REM closely at a fraction of the circuit cost, and variance-optimal shot allocation reduces ZNE's standard deviation close to the theoretically predicted amount at an unchanged shot budget.

**Recommendation.** Use REM (tensored, once qubit counts make full calibration impractical) as the default, low-cost mitigation. Add ZNE, preferably combined with REM, when gate noise is large relative to shot noise (moderate noise and above) and the extra circuit depth and shots are affordable; skip ZNE when noise is already low. For a downstream classification task, judge mitigation by its effect on expectation values and margins, not on accuracy alone.

**Further work.** Probabilistic error cancellation (unbiased, but exponential sampling cost and needs noise tomography), Clifford data regression (needs training circuits), dynamical decoupling (targets idle/coherent errors absent from this model), M3-style matrix-free REM (ref. 8) for larger qubit counts, data re-uploading for the VQC encoding (ref. 15), noise-aware VQC training (with the caveats of ref. 16), and validation on real hardware.

### D. Deviations from the plan

See `DECISIONS.md` for the full dated log. In summary: the Python environment uses CPython 3.11 via `uv` (the system default was 3.13, outside the plan's range); `pylatexenc` was added for circuit diagrams; the shot-allocation experiment's repeated circuits are batched into one Aer call per (condition, scheme, scale) rather than one call per repetition, verified to preserve independent, reproducible randomness while being far faster.
